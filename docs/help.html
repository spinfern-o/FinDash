Get your numbers in

FinDash needs one spreadsheet with a row for each month. The quickest way is the template: on the dashboard, press Download Excel template (or CSV). It comes filled in with three sample months, so you can see what goes where.

  1. Month and Year: the month's name, such as July, and the year, such as 2026.
  2. Revenue: everything that came in from sales that month.
  3. Budget: the most you planned to spend that month in total. It is a spending limit, not a sales target. Leave it blank if you don't set one.
  4. The 14 cost columns: each month's total for that cost. Leave a cost at 0 if you don't have it.

Save the file and drop it on the dashboard, or press Choose file. Excel (.xlsx) and CSV both work.

From QuickBooks Online

  1. Go to Reports and open Profit and Loss.
  2. Pick the months you want and set Display columns by to Months.
  3. Copy each month's income and cost totals into the matching template columns.

From Xero

  1. Open Reports, then Profit and Loss.
  2. Choose your date range and compare by month.
  3. Copy each month's totals into the template.

From your own spreadsheet

  • Put one month per row, with the template's column names across the top. The order of the columns doesn't matter.
  • Common alternative names are recognised: Wages or Payroll for Labor, Advertising for Marketing, Cost of goods for Cost of goods sold, Repairs for Maintenance, and so on.
  • A sheet laid out the other way round, with costs down the side and months across the top, is read too.
  • Rows that add other rows up (Total costs, Gross profit, Net income) are spotted and left out, so nothing is counted twice.

A cost that doesn't have its own column? Add it to the closest one; the cost groups below say what belongs where.

The older two-file layout (one file with month, month num, revenue and budget, and one with the expense columns) still works: add both files together.

From a photo of an invoice or receipt

For costs, you can skip typing: take a photo of a supplier invoice, a bill or a receipt, and add it to the dashboard the same way as a file. A screenshot of an emailed invoice works too.

  • Take a clear photo. The whole page, flat, in good light, with every amount in the frame.
  • Claude reads it. Each charged line is read, given in English (invoices in two languages are fine), and put in the cost line it belongs to: food and ingredients go to Cost of goods sold, takeout containers and bags to Packaging, and so on. Handwritten notes are not counted as charges, and free items are counted at zero.
  • The arithmetic is checked. Each line's quantity times price must match its amount, and the lines must add up to the printed total. If they don't, the photo is read a second time with the mistakes pointed out. An amount that was cut off or too faded to read is worked out from quantity times price and marked so you can check it.
  • You check it before anything is added. You see every line, can change the cost line any of them goes to, and choose the month (the invoice's own date by default). Nothing is added until you press Add.
  • It adds to that month. The invoice's costs are added on top of the month's figures, so only add invoices your monthly file doesn't already count. A receipt for money you took in, such as a till report, adds to revenue. Every invoice you add is listed under the import box, with a Remove button.

PDF invoices can't be read yet: open the PDF and take a screenshot of it instead.

If a file won't load

"Columns not recognised"
The file needs a Revenue column and at least one cost column such as Rent or Cost of goods sold. Check the top row holds column names, or start from the template.
".xls is the older Excel format"
Open the file in Excel and choose Save As, then Excel Workbook (.xlsx) or CSV.
"Could not open"
The file may be damaged or password-protected. Remove the password, save it again, and retry.
"Need one more file"
You added half of the older two-file layout. Add the other file, or switch to the one-file template.
The months are in the wrong order or show the wrong year
Add a Year column. Without one, every month is assumed to be in the current year.
"Photos can't be read here"
Reading photos needs this site's AI service, and the copy of FinDash you're using doesn't have it set up. Use the template instead.
"This browser can't open that kind of photo"
iPhones often save photos as HEIC, which some browsers can't open. Take a screenshot of the photo, or change your camera setting to "Most Compatible", and add that instead.
"The lines add up to … but the printed total is …"
Even after a second look, the reading doesn't match the invoice's total. Check the lines marked in red against your paper copy. Retaking the photo flatter and in better light usually fixes it.
A figure is 100 times too big or too small
Write amounts in full. 1,250.00 and 1.250,00 are both read correctly, but a short "1,25" could mean either 1.25 or 125, so it is read as 125.

What the numbers mean

Every cost sits in exactly one of four groups, so the groups add up to your total costs and revenue minus total costs is your net profit. The same explanations appear behind each ? on the dashboard.

How we handle your data

Your spreadsheet stays on your computer. It is opened and added up by your own browser. It is never uploaded, and FinDash has no accounts and no database of anyone's figures.

Four things can leave your computer, and only these:

  • When you add a photo: the photo is sent to Claude, Anthropic's AI model, to be read. FinDash does not store it; only the lines you confirm are kept, in this browser.
  • When you press Get advice: each month's totals (revenue, the four cost groups, net profit and budget) are sent to Claude, Anthropic's AI model, which writes the advice. No line items, no names, not your file. FinDash does not store them.
  • When a column name isn't recognised: the column names from your file, never the numbers, are sent so the unfamiliar ones can be matched to the right cost group. Files that use the template's names never need this.
  • If a forecasting model is offered: once you have six months on file, a button may appear under the forecast. Pressing it sends the net profit figure for each month, nothing else.

Saved in this browser: after you add a file or a photo, the worked-out monthly figures, the invoice lines you confirmed and your latest advice (not the files or photos themselves) are kept in this browser on this device, so they are there next time. They are never sent anywhere. Press Forget my data on the dashboard to remove them. On a shared computer, do that before you leave.

Printing and sharing

  • Print or save as PDF gives a clean, light report of the period on screen. In the print window, choose Save as PDF as the printer to get a file to email.
  • Export to Excel downloads every line for every month side by side, with a total column, ready for your accountant, partner or bank.
