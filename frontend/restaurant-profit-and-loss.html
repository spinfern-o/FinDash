What it shows for a restaurant

FinDash was first built for a restaurant, so its cost lines are a restaurant's: food and drink, labor, payroll tax, staff meals, packaging, card fees, rent and the rest. Each month you see:

  • Food cost: cost of goods sold (food and drink) as a share of sales.
  • Labor: wages, payroll tax and staff meals as a share of sales.
  • Prime cost: food cost plus labor, the biggest share of most restaurants' spending and the part you can change week to week. In FinDash it is the direct costs group.
  • Rent and other fixed costs: what doesn't shrink when sales do.
  • Net profit and net margin: what is left after every cost.
  • Budget vs actual: how much of the month's spending budget you used, and where you went over.
  • What needs attention: loss-making months, budget overruns and unusual costs.

Restaurant benchmarks, as a rough guide

Figures quoted across the industry, which vary a lot by concept and location. Your own trend, month by month, tells you more than any single target.

  • Prime cost: often targeted at about 60–65% of sales for full-service restaurants, and a little lower for quick-service (7shifts; Chron).
  • Food cost: commonly quoted at about 25–35% of sales, and labor at about 25–35%, higher for table service (Chron).
  • Rent and occupancy: a common rule of thumb keeps it under about 8–10% of sales (Restaurant Business). Because rent is a fixed amount, the share swings with sales, as September shows.
  • Net margin: thin. Full-service restaurants often keep only a few percent of sales as profit, around 3–5% by many estimates (Bloom Intelligence).

Get your restaurant's numbers in

  1. On the dashboard, press Download Excel template (or CSV).
  2. Fill in one row per month: sales, your budget, and the cost lines above. Copy the totals from your Profit and Loss report in QuickBooks or Xero, or from your own spreadsheet.
  3. Drop the file on the dashboard. It is read in your browser; nothing is uploaded.
  4. With a free account, add photos of supplier invoices too: each line is read, checked against the total, and added to its month once you confirm it.

Questions restaurant owners ask

What is prime cost?

Prime cost is your cost of goods sold (food and drink) plus your total labor: wages, payroll tax and staff meals. It is usually the largest part of a restaurant's spending. FinDash shows it each month as the direct costs group, as a share of sales.

What is a good prime cost for a restaurant?

Full-service restaurants often aim for about 60 to 65% of sales, and quick-service a little lower. Much above that leaves little room for rent and everything else. How your own prime cost moves from month to month matters more than any one target.

What is a good food cost percentage?

Food cost is commonly quoted at about 25 to 35% of sales, depending on the menu and the concept. FinDash shows it each month as cost of goods sold divided by sales.

What is a good net profit margin for a restaurant?

Restaurant margins are thin: full-service restaurants often keep around 3 to 5% of sales as net profit, and a slow month can easily turn into a loss when rent and other fixed costs stay the same.

Can I use the numbers from my POS or accounting software?

Yes. Copy each month's totals from your Profit and Loss report in QuickBooks or Xero, or from your POS sales reports, into FinDash's template. FinDash doesn't connect to your POS or accounting software; it reads the spreadsheet you give it.

Does FinDash replace my bookkeeper or accountant?

No. FinDash doesn't keep your books, run payroll or file taxes. It turns the monthly totals you already have into a clear picture of food cost, labor, prime cost and profit, so you can see where the month went.
